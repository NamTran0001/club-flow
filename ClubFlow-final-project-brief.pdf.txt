1. The problem
When recruiting members, a club may receive CVs through forms, review applications in
spreadsheets, and coordinate interview times in group chats, leaving information about each
application scattered across several tools. If a student applies for multiple positions and an
interviewer serves multiple departments, organizers must also compare role requirements,
availability, and rooms to avoid scheduling conﬂicts. Manual updates can cause applications to
be missed, CVs to be assigned to the wrong interviewer, unconﬁrmed schedules to be shared,
or interview feedback to be attached to the wrong application; students may also have
diﬃculty knowing the status of their applications. ClubFlow addresses fragmented recruitment
tracking and coordination by keeping application records, assignments, approved schedules,
interviewer assessments, and ﬁnal outcomes in one process with clear ownership, helping
clubs manage a campaign and applicants receive accurate information about their applications
and results.

3. Main ﬂow
3.1. Prototype purpose and entry point
The prototype shows a club Executive coordinating one recruitment campaign, from
preparing campaign details through conﬁrming interview schedules. The user is signed in as an
Executive, and all actions belong to the same club and campaign.
The three screens are connected in this order:
Create Recruitment Campaign → Application Review → Interview Scheduling.
Applicants submit their CVs after the campaign is published; those submissions become the
input to application review. In the prototype, sample applications represent submissions
received during that period. Moving from Screen 1 to Screen 2 does not automatically create
applicants or approve applications.
3.2. How the prototype ﬂow works
Step 1 — Prepare and publish a campaign in Create Recruitment Campaign. The
Executive enters the campaign name, host club, description, opening and closing dates, and
application instructions. They add departments and positions, quotas, job descriptions, and
requirements. Save as Draft stores the conﬁguration for later editing; Preview Campaign shows
what students will see. When the user selects Publish Campaign, the system checks required
ﬁelds and dates before publishing. If the information is invalid, the user stays on the page and
corrects it. After the campaign is published and receives applications, Applications opens
Screen 2 for that same campaign.
Step 2 — Review each application in Application Review. The list shows submitted
applications, applicants, positions, submission times, and statuses. The Executive searches or
ﬁlters the list, then selects Review to open an application drawer beside it. The Executive reads
the CV and statement, adds an internal note, and selects Shortlist Candidate or Reject. The
system updates only the application being reviewed. If one student applies for two positions,
the decision for one position does not change the other application. Closing the drawer returns
to the list and keeps the current ﬁlters.

Step 3 — Send eligible applications to scheduling. Once there is at least one Shortlisted
application, the Executive selects Continue to Interview Scheduling. Screen 3 receives the
applications that passed CV screening for the same campaign, along with the conﬁgured
interviewers, availability, and rooms. Only eligible applications enter scheduling. Shortlisted
means an application passed CV screening; it does not mean the applicant has been accepted
or already has a conﬁrmed interview.
Step 4 — Generate and review a schedule in Interview Scheduling. The Executive
selects a date, time window, and interview duration, then generates a draft. The system checks
available time slots, an interviewer assigned to the relevant position, both parties’ availability,
and an available room. The draft labels assignments as Proposed, applications that could not
be scheduled as Unassigned, and assignments with a scheduling conﬂict as Conﬂict. The
Executive can select Edit, Resolve, or + Assign Slot to adjust or assign an interview manually.
The system rechecks the people, time, and room after each change. Save Changes stores the
draft only.
Step 5 — Approve and conﬁrm the schedule. After applications close, all applications have
been reviewed, and the draft has been checked, the Executive selects Approve & Conﬁrm
Schedule. The system displays a summary and allows conﬁrmation only when every Shortlisted
application has a valid assignment and none remain Unassigned or in Conﬂict. After
conﬁrmation, the campaign conﬁguration and schedule become read-only. Each applicant and
interviewer can view their own conﬁrmed schedule, and Screen 3 shows the Conﬁrmed state.
The prototype ends with schedule conﬁrmation; this action does not announce recruitment
results.
3.3. After the prototype ﬂow
The three prototype screens cover campaign setup, CV screening, and schedule conﬁrmation.
After the interviews, each assigned Interviewer submits one assessment for each completed
interview through the assigned application record. The Executive records a Pass or Fail decision
for each position. The application then displays the result to the applicant and sends an in-app
notiﬁcation. The three prototype screens show the Executive's main ﬂow; the applicant
submission/results view and assigned Interviewer assessment view support the remaining
steps.
3.4. End-to-end example
Student A applies for two positions—Content and Communications—in the same campaign,
creating applications A01 and A02. The Executive reviews each CV and shortlists both
applications. When scheduling, each application must be assigned an interviewer qualiﬁed for
its position, and the two interviews cannot overlap because they belong to the same student. If
the Executive moves both interviews to 09:00–09:20, the system reports a conﬂict and blocks
approval. After moving one interview to another available slot and checking the draft again, the
Executive conﬁrms the schedule. Student A then sees two separate interviews at diﬀerent
times. This example illustrates how the ﬂow works.

3.5. Exception ﬂow
Required information is missing or the campaign is closed: show a speciﬁc error, do
not submit the application, and retain valid information already entered.
The same student applies twice for the same position in one campaign: open the
existing application; applications for other positions remain allowed.
No qualiﬁed interviewer, shared available time, or room is available: leave the
application Unassigned and show the reason so the Executive can adjust the draft; do not
reject the applicant automatically.
A schedule edit creates an overlap: identify the aﬀected interviews and block
conﬁrmation of the conﬂicting assignment.
Applications remain unscheduled: block conﬁrmation and identify the applications that
need attention; the Executive adjusts availability, interviewer, or room and regenerates the
draft.
A conﬁrmed schedule is opened: show it as read-only and disable editing and
regeneration.
4. Scope
This is the starting scope. The product owners may narrow or clarify it (section 6).
The semester version serves one club, one active recruitment campaign at a time, and
one interview date per campaign. Accounts for all three roles are provided in advance; the
application supports sign-in and role-based access. Each application has one CV screening
round, at most one interview with one interviewer, and one assessment for that interview.
Conﬁrmed schedules remain available for viewing while the application proceeds to its ﬁnal
decision.
Building
1. Create recruitment campaigns: Enter campaign details, deadlines, instructions, and a
list of departments/positions with their respective quotas. Support saving drafts,
previewing, and publishing campaigns.
2. Accept applications: Provide a ﬁxed application form containing student information, a CV
in PDF format (maximum 5 MB), a motivation statement (maximum 300 words), and
availability based on time slots provided by the club. A student may apply for multiple
positions, with a separate application required for each position. Duplicate applications for
the same position are not allowed.
3. Screen CVs: Search and ﬁlter applications, view CVs and application details in a drawer,
add internal notes, and mark each application as Shortlisted or Rejected. Maintain
application and applicant statistics, sorting, pagination, and basic CSV export on the
application list.
4. Prepare interview schedules: Allow the Executive to assign interviewers to one or more
positions, enter their available time slots, and select available rooms. Each room can host
only one interview session at a time. All interviews within a campaign must use the same
slot duration: 15, 20, or 30 minutes.

5. Propose and approve interview schedules: Schedule only Shortlisted applications by
sequentially selecting the ﬁrst available combination of time slot, interviewer, and room.
Display reasons for Unassigned applications, allow draft schedule adjustments, and support
schedule table export. Check for conﬂicts involving applicants, interviewers, and rooms
within the campaign. The schedule can only be conﬁrmed after applications have closed, all
applications have been reviewed, all required interviews have been scheduled, and no
conﬂicts remain. Once conﬁrmed, the campaign conﬁguration, applications, and schedule
become read-only.
6. Provide role-based access: Applicants can view their CV screening status and personal
interview schedules. Interviewers can only access their assigned schedules and applicants'
CVs. Executives can view and manage all applications within a campaign. CV screening
notes must not be visible to Applicants or Interviewers.
7. Evaluate candidates and send notiﬁcations: Allow Interviewers to evaluate candidates
and notify applicants of their ﬁnal results (pass/fail).
Not Building
1. Support for multiple clubs or simultaneous recruitment campaigns; self-service account
registration or recovery; university account veriﬁcation; and multi-level administrative
access control.
2. Dynamic room creation, room capacity management, university room booking, checking
schedules outside the active campaign, or large-scale schedule optimization.
3. University single sign-on (SSO).
4. AI-powered CV screening or ranking, advanced analytics dashboards, audit trails, or
customizable application forms.
5. Post-recruitment management of events, members, and internal task assignments; chat,
video calls, payments, or a dedicated mobile application.
5. Screens
These three screens show the features PA#2 needs. They are a starting point, not a picture to
copy: your team chooses the screens, the layout, the content, the colours and the ﬁle names
(index.html  is the ﬁrst page), and you may build them as static pages or with React. What
counts is that your pages deliver these features and that none of the problems below appears
in them.
Problems your pages must not have
The rubric calls this the Fix these list. Where an item names a screen or a sample value, it
describes the authors' screens. Your own pages and content only have to avoid the same
problem.
Dates do not ﬁt together. Applications close on Oct 30, 2026 on Screen 1 and on Nov 5,
2026 on Screen 2, and interviews on Screen 3 are on Oct 27, 2026, before either date. Use
one timeline on all three pages.

Screen 2 has the wrong page title. Its top bar still says "Create Recruitment Campaign ·
DRAFT MODE".
The drawer on Screen 2 covers most of the list. The application details and the list
must both stay readable at every width.
The counts on Screen 3 do not add up. The footer says 6 Proposed, 1 Conﬂict and 2
Unassigned, which makes 9, but the table has 8 rows and only 5 of them are Proposed.
The conﬂict on Screen 3 is not a conﬂict. The banner reports an overlap at 09:20, but
no other row overlaps the highlighted one.
The sample people overlap. Trần Gia Huy and Hoàng Thảo Vy appear as both applicants
and interviewers on Screen 3.
"Veriﬁed" contradicts the scope. "Veriﬁed Student", "100% veriﬁed", "Veriﬁed
Attachments" and "veriﬁed student credentials" all suggest university account veriﬁcation,
which is under Not Building.
The footer is template text. Remove "© 2025 ClubFlow", Privacy Policy, Terms of Service
and Support Center.
Screen 1 — Create Recruitment Campaign
This screen allows the Club Executive to conﬁgure a recruitment campaign by entering its basic
information, application period, recruitment departments, available positions, vacancy quotas,
and position requirements. The executive can save the campaign as a draft, preview it, or
publish it. After publishing the campaign, the executive proceeds to the application review
stage to manage incoming applications.

Screen 2 — Application Review
This screen allows the Club Executive to review applications submitted to the recruitment
campaign. The executive can view application statistics, inspect applicant proﬁles and
statements of purpose, and review attached CVs. For each application, the executive can

shortlist or reject the applicant. Shortlisted applicants are then passed to the interview
scheduling stage.
Screen 3 — Interview Scheduling
This screen allows the Club Executive to generate and review a proposed interview schedule
for shortlisted applicants. The executive can inspect interview dates, time slots, assigned
interviewers, rooms, and scheduling conﬂicts. The executive can edit assignments, resolve
conﬂicts, and export the timetable. Once all necessary assignments and conﬂicts have been
reviewed, the executive can approve and conﬁrm the schedule, completing the interview
scheduling stage.

6. Product owners
The authoring team takes the lead on the product. They decide how ClubFlow behaves and
what is in scope wherever this brief is silent or leaves room, for example which parts of the
scope can wait, or what an applicant sees after a rejection.
Ask under the post ClubFlow — questions for the product owners, which they open in the
course Facebook group, starting with #WAD24KTPM1. Do not ask in private messages: one
team's answer is everyone's answer.

Answers come within two working days, under that post. A scope decision posted there
applies to every team. Cite it in your repository when you rely on it.
What the product owners do not decide: marks, deadlines, rubrics, and what PA#2
requires: the features of its three screens and the Fix these list. Those belong to the
lecturer.
The product owners build ClubFlow like every other team and are marked the same way.
